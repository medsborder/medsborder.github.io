What MedsBorder is

MedsBorder is a free tool that shows, for the medicines and substances and the countries you enter, what we found in official lists, and links to the sources. It helps you know what to check. It does not decide what is allowed at a border.

Not legal or medical advice

Nothing on this site is legal, customs, medical or pharmaceutical advice, and using it does not create any professional relationship. Do not start, stop or change a treatment because of what you read here; talk to your doctor or pharmacist. For legal questions about a journey, ask the embassy or consulate of each country on your route, its customs service or a qualified lawyer.

Results can be wrong or incomplete

  • Laws change, and officials may apply them differently from the text. Every source shows the date we last checked it; an old date means the data may be out of date.
  • “No data” never means “allowed”. If a country or ingredient is shown as unknown, we have not found a source, and you must find the rule yourself.
  • Many entries describe a control list, not the rules for carrying medicine in your luggage. Quantity, dosage, form, a doctor’s letter, a translated prescription, a customs declaration, an import permit or your citizenship can all matter and are mostly not covered.
  • Statuses of different countries use different scales (for example “restricted” and “prescription”) and are only roughly comparable.
  • Names of ingredients, brands and synonyms may be incomplete or wrong. A custom ingredient you type is never checked; only search links are offered.
  • Transit rules can differ from entry rules, and a layover can mean passing through the country’s border control. Check each country on your route, including layovers.

Use it lawfully

This site is meant to help you follow the law, not to avoid it. It does not tell you how to hide, smuggle or disguise any substance, and it does not encourage breaking the law of any country. Penalties for carrying controlled substances can be severe, including detention and imprisonment, and ignorance of the rule is usually not a defence.
